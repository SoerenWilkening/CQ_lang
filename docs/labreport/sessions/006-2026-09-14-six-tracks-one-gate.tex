\subsection*{Goal}
Entry 005 closed with the owner's cascade compiling at \(N=2\) and declining at \(N=3\), with the loop form
scoped but not built, and with \bd{3d8q} --- the one verify-clean silent miscompile this project knows it
ships --- recorded as gaining customers from the work that had just landed. This session set out to remove
the \(N=3\) decline at whatever depth the owner writes, to take down the first wall of the loop form, and to
close \bd{3d8q} before any further widening fed it. A second aim was procedural, from the owner's instruction
that test runs be reduced to a minimal number: six increments gated \emph{once}, as one group, instead of
once per landing.

\subsection*{The headline}
\fpath{example/grover/grover_oracle_uncompute.c} with \textbf{three} \texttt{bool}s --- three guarded state
preparations with a controlled subtraction between them, no \texttt{int} workaround and no new
\texttt{cq\_*} function --- compiles as written: \textbf{exit 0, stderr 0 bytes, 35 trace lines}, strict
stage 2b \emph{``9 rail mint(s) of which 7 left allocated, 3 cqrt\_free site(s): 3 verified, 0 violation(s),
0 unproven''}, and the trace is \textbf{byte-identical} to the committed golden (both sha256
\texttt{3c1dda4a48e8d2ca\ldots}). The uninitialised spelling \texttt{bool b0;} now gives the same 35 lines ---
a prediction an earlier fixture header had written down, measured true. In that golden the two \texttt{bool}
registers appear only as \texttt{cqrt\_cnot} controls and as the control of \texttt{cqrt\_ry\_i1\_controlled};
every \texttt{cqrt\_x} is on an ancilla whose \(x \oplus x = 0\) return is what makes its free a proven one.
Second, and not visible in that file: a \emph{rolled} \texttt{bool}-controlled Ry arm or state preparation
now lowers, where \fpath{CLAUDE.md} had recorded the cost of the gap as total --- ``Grover as a rolled loop
with a bool-controlled rotation or prep compiles in NO spelling on ANY binary''.

\subsection*{The group gate --- one suite for six landings, and three things no per-track gate could see}
All six branches were merged into one worktree, built once and gated once. Three interactions surfaced that
a per-track gate cannot see \emph{by construction}, each needing two tracks present at once.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{F \(\times\) U.} Track U's seed normaliser turns one of Track F's \emph{decline} fixtures
    into Track F's \emph{accept} fixture byte-for-byte. Predicted by a reviewer, written into the gate brief
    as a named probe, measured to hold; the undef spelling of all four of Track F's customers then gave the
    same verdict as its \texttt{= 0} twin, traces byte-identical at 29/35/45 lines.
  \item \textbf{F \(\times\) P.} Two programs --- a condition \emph{tree} whose leaves are preparations'
    merge \(\varphi\)s --- decline under either track alone and \textbf{accept only together}. Stage 2b is
    strict-clean and the streams well formed, and the record states plainly that this is \textbf{cleared by
    measurement and not by review}: both tracks' rounds saw binaries on which these decline.
  \item \textbf{Track F's own retirement trigger firing.} F's round-1 fix carries a trigger naming
    \bd{wx8g} \emph{by name} as the only reason a back-edge shape was unreachable --- and \bd{wx8g} landed
    in the same merge. This was predicted and written into the gate brief as a named check \emph{before} the
    gate ran. The two named tripwire fixtures held byte-identically, but three probes built from the shapes
    Track W now admits move from ``control flow inside a loop body'' to one F1 decline and two accepts: the
    shape is reachable and the comment's premise is false. The re-derivation is recorded as \textbf{owed},
    deliberately not attempted.
\end{itemize}
The gate's first full run was \textbf{red}, 3 of 444, reproduced at \texttt{-j1} in 1.82\,s so contention was
excluded. Two failures were attributed to Track D alone by running the exact linked module on all seven
binaries --- a ruling applied to the lit layer and not to two e2e slices of the same program. Resolving them
surfaced a coverage hole and closed it: one of the two programs was pinned nowhere else in the tree, so
unregistering it alone would have dropped the decline outright; its verbatim linked module is now a fixture,
validated non-vacuous \emph{by binary mutation} --- FileCheck does not match on the oracle, where
\texttt{opt} exits 0, and matches on the merged binary. The third failure was the twin matrix's 41 moved
cells, adjudicated by cause and by unitary before anything was regenerated; the most instructive goes
\textsc{accept-identical} \(\to\) \textsc{decline-both} --- two halves that agreed because both were wrong,
which an agreement check cannot see.

\subsection*{Six instruments were measured reporting a PASS when they had measured nothing}
Each was found by the agent that owned the instrument; every one failed toward ``pass''.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \fdoc\ A vacuity checker printed \texttt{checked 0} over 29 real negatives (it matches only
    negatives naming an SSA handle). Its replacement's first \emph{universe} was too small and reported two
    false vacuous --- the same failure, reversed.
  \item \fdoc\ An injection matrix printed \texttt{0 mutations, MISSED 0, N/A 0} when invoked with \emph{no
    fixture argument}; it now exits non-zero.
  \item \fdoc\ An injection harness lied twice \emph{in the MISSED direction} first: one spelling
    re-inserted a line where it already was, the next moved a mutation across a function boundary.
  \item \fdoc\ An earlier matrix reported \textbf{45 MISSED} against fixtures that catch all of them,
    having dropped the RUN line's \texttt{--implicit-check-not} flags --- a harness measuring itself.
  \item \fdoc\ \texttt{--regenerate} silently deleted \textbf{54 lines} of hand-added Rule-13 ruling prose
    --- a ruling, its re-derived unitary, a reviewer's caveat, a retirement trigger --- and reported
    success. The sibling file's header says it is script-owned; this one's did not, and that was the whole
    difference.
  \item \fdoc\ A \textbf{0-byte \texttt{.ll} module accepts} through the passthrough early-out, so six
    scoping probes scored false accepts on nothing after a \texttt{sed} redirection truncated its own source
    on a case-insensitive filesystem. Two of three affected claims survived re-measurement; one did not.
\end{itemize}
Two structural gaps were filed with them, each explaining a past near-miss. Every corpus here is
effectively \emph{one function per module} --- where a fixture does carry two \texttt{define}s, both copies
want the \emph{same} verdict, so the missing axis is a module whose two loops want different ones. That is
why the dangling \texttt{Loop *} survived 1937 fixtures, 15 e2e entries, a complete corpus sweep, a 0-MISSED
matrix and 328 twin cells. And \emph{no hand-written \texttt{.ll} fixture can pin a diagnostic's location},
which is why a lost \texttt{!dbg} survived a green 1946-fixture run and was caught only by a per-row
full-stderr hash.

\subsection*{The owner's rulings}
Recorded in \fpath{DECISIONS.md}; two were decided on measurements that refuted the orchestrator's own
analysis.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\bd{om35}: build the \(\varphi\)-descendant predicate, not the broader rival.} The question
    put was which rule yields ``a more general method not over-declining''. Scoping measured that the rival
    flips \textbf{25} shipped decline fixtures, not the bead's \(\ge 11\), \textbf{13} of which say
    \textsc{confirmed miscompile} in their own first ten lines. All 25 rivals were built and run and all
    still decline --- but on two \emph{capability} fences this project is actively removing, so the margin
    expires on our own roadmap, and in floating point it already has none. The rule built flips \emph{zero}
    and covers the cascade family at any depth; the rival stays filed with its price.
  \item \textbf{\bd{3d8q}'s in-loop spelling declines.} The analysis put to the owner said the emitted chain
    was the only realizable circuit, the alternative requiring cloning. The implementer refuted it by
    writing the program: the state is a \emph{known preparation}, so producing it again is re-preparation,
    and that spelling already compiles. The deciding measurement: intended i.i.d.\ sampling at
    \(P(1)=\sin^2(0.2)\approx0.0395\) per trip, against \(P(1\,|\,\mathrm{prev}{=}0)\approx0.0025\) and
    \(P(1\,|\,\mathrm{prev}{=}1)\approx0.9975\) --- a chain locked onto its first outcome, with nothing in
    the trace that shows it. Two realizable circuits; the source names neither. Both unambiguous spellings
    are now pinned as accepts, making the decline a two-token fix rather than a lost capability.
  \item \textbf{A post-measurement \texttt{cq\_theta} is a capability} --- ``it just requires a
    \texttt{c\_alloc} again''. Measured: chain and fresh allocation are the same state, since the
    measurement is what disentangles the register; they differ only on a \emph{later} read of the measured
    name, where the chain is wrong. Designed and costed (0 shipped accepts move, 2 of 367 goldens shorten,
    no new symbol), not built.
  \item \textbf{\bd{z9t1}}, five clauses: the rule attaches to qubit allocation and makes no claim about the
    C variable; \emph{both} spellings compile, which forces the early placement; it is silent;
    \texttt{cq\_measure} of a never-quantum \texttt{undef} keeps declining (\bd{21mg}); it is not total.
  \item \textbf{Order.} \bd{3d8q} got a track on correctness-over-expressiveness grounds; the twin matrix
    was widened \emph{before} the loop work; and after Track L's scoping falsified \bd{xdu2}'s premise,

\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Verification run}
One merge-and-gate agent ran the session's only full suites, on the merged tree: \texttt{ctest -j6}
\textbf{red at 3/444} (497.00\,s), then, after a resolution that touched no pass source and left both
artefacts bit-identical, \textbf{green at 442/442} (448.60\,s). On that same binary,
\texttt{cq-opt-equivalence-full} compared 1993 fixtures with 0 mismatches; the corpus sweep ran to
completion rather than being closed by a tail argument, repaying two tracks' unfinished sweeps. The closing
agent rebuilt the primary once and re-ran the full suite on \texttt{main}: \textbf{442/442} in 436.73\,s,
with both rebuilt artefact hashes reproducing the gate's expected values exactly. Implementers ran
\texttt{ir-pass-lit} once at hand-off and their own fixtures and slices singly; reviewers ran no build and no
suite. \emph{Not run}: there is no simulator and none is planned (Rule 2); the non-loop twin matrices, two of which
share the regenerated-header shape of the instrument that ate 54 lines, are unaudited.

One confusion is worth recording once, because from outside it is indistinguishable from a regression. After
the landings the owner's file ``still declined'' --- and the decline was real, well formed and
source-located. The primary \fpath{build/} had simply not been rebuilt: it was holding the frozen pre-merge
oracle, three increments old. One rebuild produced the gated binary bit for bit, and the byte-identical
golden. The cheap discriminator is the artefact hash, read before believing any decline.
