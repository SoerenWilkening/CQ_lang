\subsection*{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \foverdecline\ The shape is a \textbf{third kind} of loop-carried register, and the loop layer
        knew two. \texttt{clang -O1} if-converts the second \texttt{if} into a 4.4 data select whose
        emitter mints a \emph{fresh} \texttt{\%out} per activation, so \texttt{a} crosses the back edge
        \emph{and} is re-minted every trip --- neither the rail that crosses in place nor the
        iteration-local one. \texttt{ControlDetect.cpp} had said so in words for the value arm since
        before the loop layer existed.
  \item \foverdecline\ Four walls, not two: clause (c3), X1 at round 2,
        \texttt{declineUnmarkedRolledIterLocal}'s \emph{conjunct 2} and \texttt{declineClassicalMuxes}.
        Conjunct 2 is a \emph{postcondition}, which is why reading decline strings could not find it;
        the ladder was mapped with an env-gated peel instrument, because
        \texttt{LLVMContext::diagnose} exits on the first \texttt{DS\_Error} and otherwise each wall
        costs a rebuild.
  \item \fconfirmed\ Two of the eight conjuncts exist \emph{only} to protect a diagnostic, and both
        were found by probe rather than by review: without (Z6) the \texttt{i32} accumulator lost
        \texttt{kMsgCrossIter}; without (Z8) the rotated zero-trip-guarded loop reported about the exit
        $\varphi$ instead of the loop. Both are declines either way --- the conjuncts decide
        \emph{which owner speaks}.
  \item \fconfirmed\ Rule-13 round 1 (independent, \texttt{CLEAR-WITH-ITEMS}, no P1, no P2, six P3s).
        Its sharpest: the (L3) exemption's safety argument named \texttt{railStoredToRailBank}, which
        guards the \emph{terminal} partition, while the marking unlocks the \emph{interior} one --- a
        true conclusion on the wrong ground. Also: the new stage's prescan used an idiom the
        neighbouring stage refuses in writing thirty lines away. Both fixed in source.
  \item \fconfirmed\ Every one of the eleven moved fixtures carried a retirement trigger predicting
        this increment, and \emph{every trigger named the wrong next wall}: each said the decline would
        move to \texttt{kMsgCrossIter}; it is gone instead. \textbf{A retirement trigger that names the
        next wall is only ever a lower bound on how many are left.}
  \item \foverdecline\ \bd{832l} was closed and \emph{reopened the same day, by measurement}: its
        literal subject (a loop-carried \texttt{\_Bool} mutated in place by a chained theta) is refused
        by two conjuncts and is unmoved on both binaries. The charter named it as this increment's
        item 1; the increment built the promotion for a different kind.
\end{itemize}
