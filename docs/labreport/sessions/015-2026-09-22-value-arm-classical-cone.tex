\subsection*{Goal}
Make the owner's own program compile. \bd{fn1q} recorded it as two independent over-declines --- one
in the loop condition, one in the value arm --- each provable by hand-unrolling the same source and
watching it compile, and the owner ruled that both had to be fixed. The session was briefed to build
two increments behind two fences with two independent reviews.

\subsection*{What the session found before it built anything}
The bisection in the bead is wrong in three places, and the corrections change what the work is.
Measured on a clean worktree rebuild of the binary the bead names, which came out bit-identical to
the recorded hash.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The condition wall does not exist.} An array-valued threshold with a constant arm
        compiles --- exit 0, 120 lowered lines. The bead's declining variant must have carried an
        array in the arm as well.
  \item \textbf{``Hand-unroll it and it compiles'' proves nothing.} Unrolling lets the optimiser
        const-fold a local constant array at a now-constant index and if-convert the result; the
        unrolled IR contains no value-arm branch at all. Swap in a global array at a run-time index
        and the \emph{loop-free} form declines too.
  \item \textbf{The axis is not loops, arrays or memory --- it is whether the optimiser if-converted
        the arm.} Every accepting neighbour in this family accepts because the arm became a
        \texttt{select}, which goes down a lowering that was cleared months ago. Every decline is the
        optimiser refusing to if-convert. A flat, loop-free, all-constant, four-variable arm declines;
        its two-variable sibling compiles.
\end{itemize}

The over-decline verdict survives all three corrections --- the compiler already seeds a register
from a run-time classical value --- but the owner's program turns out to sit behind \emph{four}
fences, not two. The session took that back to the owner, who chose the route and the pacing: one
increment, landed completely, rather than two rushed.

\subsection*{What landed}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Increment 1 --- the value arm's classical cone.} A tainted-condition value arm may now
        hold the pure classical computation that feeds its arithmetic link's classical operand. Four
        conjuncts admit a cone member: untainted, no write or side effect, no use outside the arm, and
        safe to execute unconditionally. The classifier proves them and \emph{records} the set; the
        emitter hoists exactly that set and re-derives nothing.
  \item \textbf{A failure banner for the pipeline script.} A compiler decline killed it under
        \texttt{set -e} and left one bare error line after a full IR dump, so it read as a broken
        script --- which is how this defect was reported, and why the decline itself went unexamined.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \foverdecline\ \textbf{A classical computation inside a quantum-controlled arm was a ``stray
        op''.} The rule existed for two real hazards: the emitter replays only the arithmetic chain
        while the flattener erases the whole arm block, so anything else is silently dropped and its
        bookkeeping pointer dangles. A cone member is neither --- it is moved into the predecessor by
        the same hoist that moves the chain, before the flatten, and it carries no bookkeeping at all.
  \item \fconfirmed\ \textbf{The increment owed the standing round-two question about itself.} The
        ``classical'' conjunct is asked at first-round taint; the second detection round can make a
        cone member quantum, which would leave a region already hoisted, merged and flattened on a
        premise the compiler no longer believes --- and a shape the classifier defers by design
        accepted silently. Fenced by recording the hoisted set and re-asking after the fixpoint, in
        both entry points. Reachability is argued, not exhibited, and is written down that way.
  \item \fdoc\ \textbf{One conjunct is defensive and has no test, and says so.} For every shape the
        detector admits, an arm dominates nothing but itself, so the ``no use outside the arm''
        conjunct is believed unreachable --- measured as such, kept because the containment rests on
        another stage's property, and given a retirement trigger rather than a confident comment.
  \item \foverdecline\ \textbf{A flat, loop-free, all-constant four-variable arm declines.} Filed
        rather than fixed (\bd{s9xi}). It is the simplest thing in the whole value family, and any
        claim about what that family supports has to account for it.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Verification run}
Full \texttt{ctest -j6} on the final binary: \textbf{473/473 in 529.47\,s}, with the load rising
from 2.9 to 72 during the run as other agents started --- quoted with its load, because a figure
taken under load is not comparable with one taken on a quiet box. Standalone lit: 2121 discovered,
2120 passed, 1 expected failure. A fixture-by-fixture A/B of the whole corpus against a
from-scratch rebuild of the pre-increment compiler --- which came out bit-identical to the hash
recorded at the previous session's close --- reports no accepting program lost, and exactly two
moved files, both of which this increment adds. The null control (the old compiler against itself)
ran first and moved nothing; a positive control confirmed the harness can see a move it is known to
have. The twin identity is asserted by a \texttt{diff} of the two lowered function bodies inside the
test itself, and that assertion was validated by perturbing one constant and watching it go red.
The Rule-8 trace check used the previous session's reviewer-built instrument, not the one this
project found unsound, and reports no dirty free, no use-after-free and no entangled rail left live
across an uncontrolled rotation.

\textbf{What was not run.} There is no simulator, so nothing here establishes that the emitted
unitary is the intended one --- this is exit codes, rail identities, emitted traces, a byte-identity
pair and analytic reasoning. The independent review ran no suite and no rebuild by instruction, and
swept 872 of the 2121 fixtures rather than all of them; its own thirteen-item list of what it did not
attack is the boundary of that clearance. The remaining three fences were not touched, so the
owner\'s program still does not compile.

  \item \bd{5zwz} --- the example the owner runs is untracked, so nothing compiles it. This session
        landed one slice for the capability it added; the general fix is still open.
\end{itemize}
