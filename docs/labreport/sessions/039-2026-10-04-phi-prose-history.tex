\subsection*{Goal}
\bd{test\_C\_libtooling-v7oh}, filed at the close of entry 038, asked for the retired
data-operand spelling \texttt{x = cq\_phi(x, $\theta$)} in comments to be migrated to the
register-free \texttt{cq\_phi($\theta$)}, site by site. Before any edit, each site was read
against the IR or code it describes.

\subsection*{What landed}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \commit{5b98dfe0} --- \bd{test\_C\_libtooling-v7oh} --- comment and
  documentation text only. Three sites that describe current programs were respelled: the
  onward-control phase arm in two fixture headers
  (\fpath{control\_classical\_call\_arm\_accept\_onward\_ctl\_phase.ll},
  \fpath{control\_swap\_switch\_datamux\_onward\_ctl\_phase.ll}) and in the
  \fpath{tests/e2e/CMakeLists.txt} note on \fpath{onward\_ctl\_phase\_caller.c}, whose code
  already reads \texttt{if (y > 50) cq\_phi(0.25)}. The two fixtures without a
  \texttt{3qlq} note got one. CLAUDE.md's history paragraph now says that
  register-form \texttt{cq\_phi} spellings in comments and fixture headers are pre-3qlq
  history.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Findings}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The premise of \bd{test\_C\_libtooling-v7oh} was mostly wrong. Unlike the
  \texttt{cq\_theta} value form (entry 038), whose in-place respelling gives the same IR,
  the register form of \texttt{cq\_phi} is a different construct, and the prose around it
  depends on it. Measured: 378 lines match
  \texttt{cq\_phi(<identifier>, \ldots)}. Of the 149 fixtures among them, 147 already
  carry a \texttt{3qlq} migration note (51 ``uncontrolled $\to$ deleted'', 17 ``tainted
  arm $\to$ void call'', 65 \texttt{prep\_a2} notes on re-derived checks, re-spelled
  diagnostics or a hand decision, 14 others), and their header
  prose is about the swap route: routed base, loop-carried merge $\varphi$,
  \texttt{cqrt\_cswap}. Example: \fpath{control\_swap\_inloop\_diamond.ll} reads ``a
  loop-CARRIED routed base'' directly above the C line. Respelling that line to
  \texttt{if (q > 5) cq\_phi(b)} would contradict the paragraph. Pass-source comments (57
  lines) describe diagonal-alias, arg-0 eq-fold or classical-lift mechanics, or symptoms
  measured on the old surface. Slice prose (74 lines) is already marked as OLD IDIOM,
  RETIRED or ``was''. The \texttt{tools/} and \texttt{docs/} hits (65) are the deliberately
  kept retired \texttt{register} generator surface and its documentation. The owner ruled
  to keep these as history.
  \item The first draft of one added note claimed that an uncontrolled in-body
  \texttt{cq\_phi(0.1)} is a deleted global phase. Under a tainted break that claim is
  unmeasured, so the note now cites only measured fixtures
  (\fpath{lowering\_reject\_break\_phase\_answer.ll},
  \fpath{lowering\_reject\_break\_phase\_answer\_kickback.ll}).
  \item Seen in passing, not changed: the fixtures respelled above still describe their
  arm as a ``routed phase arm'' (\texttt{cqrt\_cswap}), although their CHECK lines pin
  kickback. This is the same pre-3qlq mechanism prose that the \texttt{3qlq} notes cover.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Verification run}
Every changed line in \texttt{ir-pass/} and \texttt{tests/} is a \texttt{;} or \texttt{\#}
comment, and none is a RUN or CHECK line. One lit invocation covered the 4 touched
fixtures, and all passed. The batch gate followed, because fixtures and a
\texttt{CMakeLists.txt} changed: \texttt{ctest -LE maintenance -j6} 586 of 586 passed, \texttt{cq-opt-equivalence-full} compared 2537 fixtures with 0 mismatches, \texttt{update\_code\_map.py --check} clean. No mutants. The maintenance label and the full
\texttt{ctest} were not run.
