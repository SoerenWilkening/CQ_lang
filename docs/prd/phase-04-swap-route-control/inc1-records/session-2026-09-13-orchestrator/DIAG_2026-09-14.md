## Which complaint is a shipped fence and which is a gap — plainly

* **Complaint 1 (the owner's file declines) is a KNOWN, FILED OVER-DECLINE with
  an owner ruling pending: bd `om35`.** It is not a new defect and not a
  regression — the file compiled at 09:30 and stopped compiling because the
  third region was uncommented. Under the 2026-09-11 ruling (*"our end-goal is
  to not decline any code that will lead to a valid quantum circuit"*) it is a
  **defect to remove**, and the removal is R-2′, whose cost (≥11 shipped decline
  fixtures flip) is exactly why `om35` says *owner ruling required*. Nothing new
  needs filing; `om35` needs a decision.
* **Complaint 2 ("only compiles on initialized variables") is FOUR-FIFTHS a
  deliberate fence and ONE-FIFTH a gap.** Rows i/iv are the shipped `21mg` undef
  fence; rows ii/v are the shipped F1 `xy9n` fence; both refuse programs that
  are UB in C, and neither is a defect. **The gap is row iii**: the *same*
  `undef` that is loudly refused at a measurement and at a controlled prep is
  **silently accepted** at an uncontrolled `cq_theta` and materialises as
  `cqrt_alloc_i1(1)`. Two unfiled items fall out — the F1 message's missing
  `undef` arm, and that three-site asymmetry — and both want an owner ruling
  before code (lesson L7: three sites, three answers, nobody has ruled).
